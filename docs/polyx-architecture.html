Architecture sketch · polyx

An expert system
that mines itself

polyx reads the journal an agent already writes, synthesises rules whose support is measured against that same history, and hands the ones with own evidence to a gate. The gate's overridden denials are the only labelled counterexamples in the system — which is what lets a rule be retired rather than merely accumulated.

Components

Inside the miner

The chain below is polyness as built, with module names as they appear on disk. Two things in it are load-bearing and easy to miss: the alphabet is settled before any number is computed, and provenance governs what may be proposed without limiting what may be scored.

  • ownHolds in at least 60% of this project's own instances. A proposal is a normative act, and this is the only branch entitled to make one.
  • borrowedFails here, holds in three or more other projects. Shown when asked for, never by default — otherwise the tool imports an opinion under the appearance of a finding.
  • neitherNo support anywhere in the corpus. Refused as a proposal at any confidence, and still fully scoreable, because proposing is a claim and scoring is an observation.
Instantiation · banking CRM

Two rule families that must not be confused

The dev-tool domain has one kind of rule: a constraint on a step you cannot take back. A CRM has two, and they need different evidence standards and different fates. Collapsing them is how a recommender ends up enforcing, and how an obligation ends up as a suggestion.

Month-one deliverable

Policy archaeology, before any agent is deployed

The mined rule set and the written policy manual are two descriptions of the same institution, and they disagree. Diffing them is a compliance product that needs no agent-assist story at all — and it is the own / borrowed / neither verdict wearing a different hat.

What exists, and what polyx has to add

polyness already implements most of Figure 2 against Claude Code journals. The honest gap is everything the CRM domain makes contestable — the alphabet, the notion of consequence, and outcomes that arrive months late.

The risk to design around. Once the gate starts denying, the journal is generated under enforcement, and the support figures that justified a rule stop describing the world the gate now sees. Offline scores do not predict closed-loop behaviour. The likely mitigation is a held-out unenforced arm — a slice of traffic the gate observes but never blocks — which costs something real and should be priced into the pilot rather than discovered in month four.

The threshold problem. Every floor in thresholds.mjs was set from one corpus and is pinned by a test against that same corpus, which is circular, and the code says so in its own comment. A second domain is the only cure. That argues for running the CRM pilot early, and for treating its first thresholds as provisional rather than tuned.
